The first SUNDIAL · Bosa, Sardinia

La Meridiana

SUNDIAL: La Meridiana is the name of Bosa's summer event.

For seven days around the summer solstice, nine old solar panels on walls across Bosa draw their day of sun. Each image forms live through the day and is sealed at Bosa's sunset. After that, nothing can change it.

You can only see it there, standing in front of the panel, on your own phone, through the panel's own Wi-Fi.

A visit

Walk from wall to wall after the light

  1. Scan the QR code on the flyer or on any panel's wall plate. The map opens on your mobile data: the route, the nine panels, which are awake now, and where you are.
  2. Start at the girasole, the first stop: a young sunflower turning with the sun beside a chalkboard. Add your mark, ochre for sun or blue for shade, and write the time beside it. The board is photographed and wiped at sunset.
  3. At a panel, read the time from its shadow. Then join its Wi-Fi, panel-01 to panel-09. Its page opens by itself.
  4. Stay connected, even if your phone says there is no internet. There isn't, by design.
  5. Watch today's dial forming, or see it sealed after sunset. Tap it for the day's facts. Below it are the earlier days of the week.
  6. Sign the panel's visitors' book: under the dial, a short form where you can leave one line about the day, with a first name or town if you like. Each panel's book is read the next day, and the lines chosen appear beside that day's dial, on the panel and on this website.
  7. Leave the Wi-Fi, back to the map, on to the next panel.

After the week

One print, and nine sundials that stay

The 63 dials become one four-colour screen print, made in Bosa: the whole event on a single sheet, with its name and dates. It is the artwork; the web sheet is its record. Details of how to order a print will follow after the event.

The panels stay on their walls. They keep telling the time with their shadows, and keep recording their sun all year, published here every week (see Sun). Between events, a panel's Wi-Fi page shows the archive of past events.

Your visit and your data

Counted, never identified

A panel tells phones apart only within one hour, so it can count visitors as dots on the dial. It keeps no names, numbers or addresses, and forgets everything each night when it switches off.

Each panel's visitors' book keeps only what you write in it: one line, and a first name or town if you choose to give one.

How a panel is built

For wall owners and the curious

A retired 185 W panel, a rod at 50° bolted to its frame, lines on the glass, and a small sealed box behind it with a board powered only by the panel. Stand-offs turn the panel to face due south whatever the wall does. Nothing is drilled into the glass, and everything can be taken down.
